# Style Contracts

Identity comes from the current confirmed character package. Style comes from the selected contract and its bundled style-lock image. Never merge a sample character into the user's identity.

## Shared pixel contract

- Construct the character and objects as HD pixel art from the start.
- Use fine intentional pixel clusters, controlled stair-stepped edges, grouped hair strands, and deliberate pixel shading.
- Keep silhouettes readable and pixel density consistent across a series.
- Use a warm handmade retro indie-game illustration language.
- Do not use chunky 8-bit, a pixel filter over normal anime art, 3D toys, plastic rendering, photorealism, vector-perfect curves, or generic anime polish.

## 16:9 article contract

### Cover

Use a warm, simple pixel scene such as cream paper, light wood, or a restrained desk. Keep one topic, one character action, and one to three relevant props. Use a short title or a few labels only. Do not create a full room, a dense poster, or paragraphs of text.

### Body

Use the approved body style: warm white or ivory surface, restrained pixel border, and generous breathing room. For a `scene`, use one character, one core object, one action, and one visible result. For `process`, `checklist`, `comparison`, or `data`, prioritize large readable information structure and use a small or absent character. All non-cover images in one article share background, border, and pixel treatment.

## 3:4 knowledge-card contract

- Use a nearly solid warm ivory surface and one continuous pixel outer frame.
- Use dark cocoa or charcoal for structure and readable Chinese body text.
- Use one muted accent family; allow only a tiny semantic accent when needed.
- Use retro pixel display type for a title or keyword, bold clear module labels, and readable Chinese sans-serif body text.
- Use solid primary containers and dashed secondary annotations with consistent corners, line weight, and shallow pixel shadows.
- Build the text grid and vertical bands before placing the character.
- Keep the card information-rich but scannable; never turn it into a room scene, HTML magazine, PPT SmartArt, or a large mascot poster.

The card contract controls visual grammar, not exact wording, sample poses, or sample identity. The source and `visual_plan.exact_text` control content.

## Series lock

After the first representative page passes, use it as the look-lock reference for remaining pages in the same route. Keep frame, paper, type roles, panel language, pixel density, and palette logic stable. Change only content, layout mold, character action, and article-specific props.
